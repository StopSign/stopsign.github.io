Kill the Lich

Game 15Playtime: about 3 months, with a NG+ to repeat further

About

Won 3rd in "Best New Web Games" on /r/incremental_games, 2025.

Kill the Lich is a minimalist incremental game centered around time compression and resource propagation. Your resources build automatically over time, but their growth accelerates when you optimize their downstream flow ratios, and you can manually push them along to go even faster. Each action grants attributes that reduce the requirements of other actions, allowing you to push further. Eventually, you can head north to Fight the Lich's Forces in an automated Phase 2, gathering three vital resources that make your next loop faster and unlock new goals.

This game is now published on Steam as a free game (link here), while remaining on the website link as well. The game is the same beetween versions and the savefile can be exported/imported between them, though the steam has the premium currency and premium upgrades.

No AI was used in this game, or any of the earlier ones.

Thoughts

My most ambitious project (at this time), created after a lot of thinking about what I like in idle games and the games I've played and created, while focused on the concept of propagation. This game is in-between Idle Loops and Nanospread in design, with a core idea as well that I wanted to visually see the progress, without a ton of submenus to switch between. The compression of each loop is also heavily inspired by Increlution.

Releasing the game on Steam was an incredible achievement for me, though the reactions weren't quite as I wanted, they were about what I expected. The game ultimately is not very beginner friendly. Still, I am proud of it.

Idle Loops

Game 13Playtime: about 4 weeks (more with dmchurch's version)

About

Idle Loops is a game where you are in a time loop, and you have to go further and further each time in the time loop, keeping knowledge gained and talent between loops, to permanently get a little further every single repeat.

The link is to the original game that I created, but I have had multiple developers extend the base game and continue it on their own, with significantly updated content and features. The best version to play is dmchurch's Loops, and then a shorter but different experience at Squirrel Loops

This game turned into a subgenre of over 15 games that were inspired by it. Some of my favorites are Stuck in Time and Increlution on Steam.

I won the 2018 Most Interesting New Mechanic award in /r/incremental_games for this one.

Thoughts

I was inspired to this game from doing Autohotkey scripting of the idle games ItRtG and NGU, and I wanted to capture that feeling of small tweaks to a complicated yet idle process rather than actually playing the complicated process manually.

This game was a huge step in my idle game skills and reputation, and continued to be different than my other games, as what I like to do is create idle games exclusively using features and mechanics that I haven't seen in any other idle games. I stil think that there is an enourmous amount of interesting formulas to discover in idle games, as it is a unique genre set aside from other gaming.

This game drastically improved my coding ability in creating idle games, as I was figuring out data-driven development to separate the game data from the game engine, creating a system of code that made it very easy to add new actions.

This is the first time I created a discord community, which ended up being a *massive* deal and taught me a lot. The outpouring from the community was incredible, and I am so thankful to all the players who debugged, who gave me code and suggestions, and the legendary developers who continued the game after I put it down.

It took me a long time to realize, but one of the major draws of this game is the story element. The story itself is heavily inspired by the book Mother of Learning, an extremely well-written time loop fiction.

Feature Quest

Game 2

About

A slow clicker game.

This game has saving, and the idle elements work in another tab.

~30 hours of work or so.

The first upgrade costs 500 resource1_1, so keep clicking "Exert Yourself". More unlocks with more resource1_1.

The game picks up a lot on the 15000 costing upgrade to unlock Stroll. It has the upgrade price reduction carryover from the first attempt.

Feel free to open the console and type resource1_1 += 100000

Thoughts

I actually split my javascript into multiple files! I did it poorly though.

For this game, I wanted to make a clicker that you only needed to click every few seconds, instead of with an auto-clicker.

This was also an experiment with polynomial growth, and showing less elements to start.

I wanted it to be a choice between getting X, X^2, or a lesser amount of both. Practice vs Application, if you will.

Instead I learned that balance is kind of hard to maintain unless the balance is built-in to the game.

A different lesson I learned here was to build around a theme - my unnamed resources aren't interesting.

Another lesson is that good pacing takes planning. This game has terrible pacing - it's much too slow.

Yet another is that explanations might be important: The block that contains Exert Yourself is active play, the block that contains Take it Easy is idle play.

I abandoned it because it was too much work to add another piece for not a lot of play. Again.
